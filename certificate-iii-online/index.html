Four Certificate III courses online and how to tell them apart

A Certificate III is often the first nationally recognised qualification people pick up once they are already working. It sits at Level 3 on the Australian Qualifications Framework, and it is built around the practical side of a job rather than theory for its own sake. The harder question is usually not whether to study at this level, but which Certificate III actually matches the work in front of you.

3CIR Online offers four Certificate III courses online. They share the same level and the same entry requirement, but they are written for very different people.

Certificate III in Business (BSB30120)

This one is for routine administrative, clerical and operational work that uses technology. Training.gov.au describes it as a qualification for people in business services roles doing routine procedural, clerical, administrative or operational tasks. The course has 13 units, 6 core and 7 elective, covering communication, critical thinking, inclusive work practices and workplace safety, with electives that can be matched to your role.

It suits someone in a reception, admin, records or customer-facing office position who wants a qualification that reflects what they already do.

Certificate III in Entrepreneurship and New Business (BSB30220)

This course is for people starting or running a business as a sole trader or contractor, and for people supporting a new venture inside a larger organisation. Its ten units cover researching an opportunity, preparing business proposals, finances and compliance.

If you have an idea you are testing, or you have recently gone out on your own, this is the Certificate III that speaks to your situation.

Certificate III in Investigative Services (CPP30619)

This is the specialist option of the four. It is written for investigators responsible for factual investigation and inquiry work or covert surveillance operations. Across 16 units it covers investigation plans, locating people, interviews and statements, covert surveillance, reports and court evidence.

It is aimed at private investigators, inquiry agents and people working towards that kind of role.

Certificate III in Work Health and Safety (BSB30719)

This course is for people who carry out work health and safety duties alongside their main job. Think of the team member who has become the safety contact for a site, a warehouse or an office. Its 11 units cover WHS laws, hazard identification, risk control, consultation and emergencies.

What the four have in common

All four are studied online at your own pace, and assessment is made up of practical tasks with no exams. The entry requirement is the same for each: a Certificate III or higher, or completing the entry check that covers language, literacy, numeracy and digital skills. Enrolment is open to Australian citizens, permanent residents and New Zealand citizens living in Australia.

A simple way to choose

Start with the work, not the title. Ask yourself where most of your week goes:

  • Keeping an office or service running smoothly points to the Certificate III in Business.
  • Building or supporting a new venture points to the Certificate III in Entrepreneurship and New Business.
  • Fact-finding, inquiries or surveillance work points to the Certificate III in Investigative Services.
  • Being the person others come to about safety points to the Certificate III in Work Health and Safety.

Each course page lists every unit, so it is worth reading the unit list for your top choice before deciding. If you find most of the units describe things you already do or will soon be asked to do, you have probably found the right fit.
